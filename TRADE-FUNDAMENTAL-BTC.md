## Monetary Economics & Supply

### The 21 Million Hard Cap
Bitcoin's total supply is permanently capped at 21,000,000 BTC. As of May 2026, approximately **20.03 million BTC** have been mined — representing **95.4% of total supply ever to exist**. The remaining ~970,000 BTC will be released over the next ~120 years through exponentially declining block rewards, with the final satoshi mined around 2140.

### Post-Halving Inflation Rate
The April 2024 halving (block 840,000) cut the block subsidy from **6.25 BTC → 3.125 BTC**. Bitcoin's annualised supply inflation rate is now approximately **0.84%** — lower than gold (~1.5–2.0% annual mine supply growth) and far below fiat currencies. This places Bitcoin in a class of monetary scarcity unprecedented in financial history.

### Stock-to-Flow (S2F)
- **Stock:** ~20.03M BTC in circulation
- **Annual Flow:** ~164,250 BTC newly mined per year (3.125 BTC × 6 blocks/hr × 8,760 hrs)
- **S2F Ratio:** ~122 — meaning it would take approximately 122 years of current mining output to replicate the existing supply. Gold's S2F is estimated at ~60–70. Bitcoin's S2F now exceeds gold's, making it the scarcest programmable monetary asset by this measure.

### Halving Cycle Dynamics
Each halving has historically created a supply-demand imbalance: miners produce fewer new coins, but ETF demand and spot buying continue. Post-2024 halving, daily net new supply is approximately **450 BTC/day**, while spot Bitcoin ETFs alone have absorbed multiples of that on active trading days. The structural supply squeeze is a primary medium-term bullish fundamental.

## Network Health

### Hash Rate (Security)
Bitcoin's network hashrate has surpassed **1.01 ZH/s (zettahashes per second)** — a milestone that reflects massive industrial-scale mining infrastructure. This is the highest in Bitcoin's history and represents an enormous cumulative investment in network security. Attacking the Bitcoin network would require controlling 51%+ of this hash power — a feat estimated to cost tens of billions of dollars in hardware and electricity per day, making it economically irrational.

### Mining Difficulty
Network difficulty stands at approximately **142–146 trillion** — also at or near all-time highs. The difficulty adjustment algorithm (DAA) recalibrates every 2,016 blocks (~2 weeks), ensuring blocks are found on average every 10 minutes regardless of hash power. The high difficulty signals robust miner participation and long-term security commitment despite compressed per-unit margins.

The **MVRV Ratio** (Market Value ÷ Realized Value) compares Bitcoin's market capitalisation to its "realized cap" — the aggregate value of all coins at the price they last moved on-chain. This is Bitcoin's closest analog to a price-to-book ratio.

## Competitive Moat & Market Position

### First-Mover Advantage & Brand
Bitcoin is the world's oldest, most recognisable, and most liquid cryptocurrency. Its brand — synonymous with "crypto" in mainstream consciousness — carries an immeasurable Lindy effect: every year Bitcoin survives and grows, the probability of its long-term survival increases.

### Liquidity Supremacy
Bitcoin trades on hundreds of exchanges globally, 24/7/365. Daily spot trading volumes routinely exceed $30–50B. No other digital asset comes close to this liquidity depth. Institutional execution at scale — pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, ETFs — requires this level of market depth. It is a self-reinforcing moat.

### Bitcoin Dominance

## Risk Factors

### 1. Regulatory Risk (Global)
While U.S. regulation has improved dramatically, significant regulatory risk remains in the EU (MiCA implementation nuances), China (ongoing ban), and emerging markets. A coordinated G20 crackdown — while unlikely — would be materially negative. The U.S. political environment is currently favorable but could shift.

### 2. Competing Cryptocurrencies
Ethereum, Solana, and other Layer 1 blockchains offer programmability and smart contract functionality that Bitcoin lacks natively. As DeFi and tokenisation mature, institutional capital could diversify to multi-asset crypto portfolios, reducing Bitcoin's dominance share over time.

### 3. Quantum Computing Threat
Large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computers could theoretically break Bitcoin's elliptic curve cryptography (ECDSA), which secures private keys. The threat is not imminent (most estimates: 10–20+ years away), and Bitcoin can be upgraded to quantum-resistant cryptography if consensus is reached — but the upgrade coordination risk is non-trivial.

### 4. Mining Economics & Centralization
At current hashprices (~$23–28/PH/s/day) and average production costs (~$37,856/BTC), many smaller miners are operating at or below profitability. This could accelerate consolidation among industrial-scale operators, increasing geographic and corporate mining concentration — a long-term decentralisation concern.

### 5. Energy & ESG Criticism
Bitcoin's proof-of-work consensus mechanism consumes significant electricity. ESG-driven investment mandates could limit some institutional participation. The counternarrative — that Bitcoin increasingly uses stranded/renewable energy — is gaining traction but has not yet fully neutralised the ESG headwind.

### 6. Volatility as a Feature and Bug
Bitcoin's annualized volatility (~50–70%) makes it challenging as a stable store of value in the short term and limits its use as a medium of exchange for price-sensitive transactions. This is partially structural and may not resolve without a significantly higher, more stable market cap.

## Fundamental Weaknesses

1. **Compressed Miner Economics:** Average production cost has risen to ~$37,856/BTC post-halving while hashprice has fallen to ~$23–28/PH/s/day. Prolonged price weakness below miner cost of production historically creates sell pressure as miners liquidate reserves, which could amplify downside scenarios.

2. **Moderate Valuation — Not a Screaming Buy:** MVRV Z-Score at ~1.2 and exchange reserves at multi-year lows are bullish signals, but they indicate Bitcoin is no longer in the deeply undervalued zone of 2022–2023. Upside is meaningful but not asymmetric at current levels in the way it was at sub-$20K prices. NVT data suggests fair-to-moderately-valued, not deeply discounted.

3. **Native Programmability Gap vs. Competitors:** Bitcoin's deliberate simplicity limits its participation in the fastest-growing crypto verticals (DeFi, NFTs, tokenised real-world assets, smart contracts). While Layer 2 solutions (Lightning, RGB, Stacks) partially address this, Ethereum and Solana have multi-year head starts in developer ecosystems and total value locked (TVL), creating a potential long-term market share ceiling for Bitcoin's dominance.
