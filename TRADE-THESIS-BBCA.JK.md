**Target Price (Bull): IDR 10,000–12,000**
**Probability: 30%**

### Bull Case Drivers

**1. MSCI Forced Selling Approaches Exhaustion — Supply-Demand Reversal (Medium-High probability)**
MSCI reduced Indonesia's index weight by 16bps (0.72% → 0.56%), triggering mechanical selling of ~IDR 1.8B in passive fund rebalancing. This selling is finite and transitory. Historical precedent (Indonesia 2018 EM crisis, South Korea and Taiwan de-listing events) shows that when MSCI-driven mechanical selling exhausts, the affected stocks frequently experience sharp recoveries as value buyers step in with no remaining forced seller. If the MSCI rebalancing is near completion, the largest headwind for BBCA's price becomes a tailwind as foreign flows reverse.

**2. Rupiah Stabilization → Multiple Re-Rating (Medium probability)**
Rupiah near IDR 17,100/USD is historically depressed. Bank Indonesia's intervention track record suggests the rate will stabilize or recover, particularly if the US Federal Reserve begins cutting rates (reducing USD strength) or if Indonesian current account dynamics improve. A Rupiah recovery from 17,100 to 15,500 would represent a ~10% currency tailwind for foreign investors holding BBCA, effectively making the stock 10% cheaper in USD terms even before the IDR-denominated price moves.

**3. P/E Re-Rating to Historical Median (High probability over 24 months)**
At 12.58x P/E with profits growing at +4–5% per year, BBCA cannot rationally remain at this discount to its own historical median (24.37x) indefinitely. If profits reach IDR 62–65T by FY2027 (a conservative +4–5% growth trajectory), a re-rating to even 18x P/E would imply a stock price of IDR 10,800–11,700 — an 71–86% gain from current levels. The bull case does not require an optimistic earnings scenario — just a re-normalization of the multiple.

**4. Domestic Fund Absorption → Price Floor Established (Medium-High probability)**
Indonesian domestic investors (pension funds, insurance, retail) are net buyers at these valuations. As foreign selling slows, domestic accumulation will establish a firmer technical floor. The 4,820 IDR 52-week low likely represents a price point where domestic buyers were absorbing all available supply — a structural demand zone.

**5. Indonesia Macro Stabilization (Medium probability)**
If Indonesia stabilizes its fiscal position, the Rupiah finds a floor, and the World Bank's growth concerns prove premature, the narrative around Indonesia could shift from "distressed EM" to "recovery opportunity." BBCA, as the bellwether of Indonesian economic health, would be the primary beneficiary of any positive macro re-assessment.
