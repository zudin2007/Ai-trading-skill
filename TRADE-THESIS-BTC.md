## Bull Case

### 1. MVRV Near/Below 1.0 — Historically the Strongest Buy Zone in Bitcoin History

The Market Value to Realized Value (MVRV) ratio compares Bitcoin's market capitalization (~$1.264T) to its realized capitalization (~$1.28T — the aggregate cost basis of all BTC at last movement). An MVRV of approximately 1.0 means the average holder is at or near breakeven. In every prior Bitcoin cycle, MVRV dipping to or below 1.0 has marked the absolute bottom or deep value accumulation zone — never the beginning of sustained further downside. In 2018-2019, MVRV bottomed near 0.7. In 2022, it touched 0.76. At ~1.0 today, the market is within the historical "maximum opportunity" band. If this is not a cycle failure (i.e., if Bitcoin does make another leg higher), entries near MVRV 1.0 have historically produced the highest risk-adjusted returns in the asset class.

The Senate Banking Committee cleared it on May 14, 2026 (15-9 vote), and it was placed on the Senate Legislative Calendar (Calendar No. 423) on June 1, 2026 — formally eligible for full Senate floor consideration. The bill formally divides digital asset oversight between the SEC and CFTC and would codify Bitcoin's commodity status under federal law. Galaxy Research estimates a 60-75% probability of the bill becoming law in 2026, with a possible presidential signature during the week of August 3. Regulatory clarity is identified by Bernstein and Bitcoin Magazine as the primary remaining unlock for the next wave of institutional capital. Passage would reduce legal uncertainty that has kept many institutional allocators sidelined — a structural demand catalyst with no prior equivalent in Bitcoin's history.

### 3. Hash Rate at All-Time High + Healthy Miner Margins — The Network Has Never Been More Secure

Bitcoin's hash rate has reached 1.01+ ZH/s (zettahashes per second), an all-time high. This reflects the single most important security metric in proof-of-work: the cost to attack the network is greater than ever before. Critically, miners are profitable — at $63,208 vs. a break-even of ~$37,856, they are operating at a 67% margin above production cost. This matters for two reasons: (1) miners are not forced sellers at current prices — there is no capitulation pressure from the supply side; and (2) a healthy mining ecosystem means no sudden hash rate collapse that could trigger a confidence crisis. The previous post-halving periods where price was near miner break-even (2022: BTC near $18K, break-even ~$15K) saw distressed miner selling accelerate downtrends. That condition does not currently exist.

### 4. Exchange Reserves at 7-9 Year Lows — A Structural Supply Squeeze Is Underway

Exchange-held Bitcoin reserves (~2.21M BTC) are at their lowest levels since 2017-2018. The trend has been consistent and sustained: Bitcoin is moving off exchanges into cold storage at a rate not seen in nearly a decade. This is structurally bullish because exchange reserves represent the pool of immediately sellable supply. When that pool shrinks, it takes progressively larger amounts of buy-side capital to move the price upward — but it also means that any new demand shock (ETF inflows, CLARITY Act passage, macro risk-on rotation) would meet a thin order book, creating potential for sharp upside price discovery. The supply leaving exchanges is broadly interpreted as long-term holders accumulating and removing coins from circulation — the opposite of distribution behavior.

### 5. ETF Infrastructure ($53-62B Cumulative AUM) — An Institutional Demand Floor Now Exists That Did Not Exist in Prior Cycles
